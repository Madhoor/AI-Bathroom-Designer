# 3D Architectural Audit & Renderer Specification

Reference: KOHLER Projects Lookbook 2024 (Renders on Pages 14, 16, 18, 24, 26, 28, 30, 32).

---

## A. Current Coordinate Contract

The KOHLER AI Designer standardizes on a metric, right-handed $Z$-up Cartesian coordinate system:

- **Origin $(0, 0, 0)$**: Located at the center of the room on the finished floor surface.
- **X-Axis (Width)**: Spans $[-W/2, +W/2]$ along the primary lateral dimension (West to East).
  - For the reference 8 ft ($2.4384\text{ m}$) room: $X \in [-1.2192\text{ m}, +1.2192\text{ m}]$.
- **Y-Axis (Depth)**: Spans $[-D/2, +D/2]$ along the room depth (South to North).
  - For the reference 6 ft ($1.8288\text{ m}$) room: $Y \in [-0.9144\text{ m}, +0.9144\text{ m}]$.
  - **South Wall** ($Y = -D/2 = -0.9144\text{ m}$): Inward normal points $+Y$ (into the room).
  - **North Wall** ($Y = +D/2 = +0.9144\text{ m}$): Inward normal points $-Y$ (into the room); accommodates the primary entrance doorway.
  - **West Wall** ($X = -W/2 = -1.2192\text{ m}$): Inward normal points $+X$.
  - **East Wall** ($X = +W/2 = +1.2192\text{ m}$): Inward normal points $-X$.
- **Z-Axis (Vertical Elevation)**: Spans $[0, H]$.
  - For the reference 9 ft ($2.7432\text{ m}$) room: $Z \in [0, 2.7432\text{ m}]$.
  - $Z = 0$: Finished floor plane.
  - $Z = H$: Finished ceiling plane.
- **Three.js World Contract**:
  - Three.js defaults to $Y$-up; `camera.up.set(0, 0, 1)` establishes the canonical architectural $Z$-up coordinate space.

## B. Current Fixture Orientation Contract

- **DesignState Placements**:
  - Stored as `position: { x, y, z }` and `rotation: { x, y, z }` (Euler angles in degrees around the vertical axis).
  - Placements generated by `generatePlacementCandidates()` currently test discrete angles ($0^\circ, 90^\circ$).
- **Normalized 3D Asset Contract (`manifest.json` & GLB files)**:
  - Source CAD files from KOHLER are processed through `build_3d_catalogue.py`:
    - Native bounds are scaled from inches to meters ($0.0254$).
    - A $-90^\circ$ rotation around $X$ is baked into the GLB.
    - Grounding is applied: lowest vertex sits at $Z=0$.
    - Centering is applied: geometry centroid is centered at $X=0, Y=0$.
  - In the resulting normalized asset coordinate frame:
    - $X \in [-W_{\text{local}}/2, +W_{\text{local}}/2]$: lateral fixture width.
    - $Z \in [0, H_{\text{local}}]$: vertical fixture height ($Z=0$ is the floor/mounting contact).
    - $Y \in [-D_{\text{local}}/2, +D_{\text{local}}/2]$: fixture depth.
    - For sanitary fixtures (toilets, bidets, vanities):
      - $+Y_{\text{local}}$ is the **BACK** (tank, cistern, or wall-mounting interface; highest $Z$).
      - $-Y_{\text{local}}$ is the **FRONT** (bowl, seat, and user approach zone).

## C. Toilet Diagnosis (`29777IN-0`)

- **Observed Problem**:
  - The Innate smart toilet `29777IN-0` appeared backwards in the browser render: its flat tank/cistern faced forward into the room, while the elongated bowl pointed directly into the South wall.
- **Root Cause Analysis**:
  1. The toilet is placed near the South wall at $Y \approx -0.65\text{ m}$ with `rotation: { x: 0, y: 0, z: 0 }`.
  2. In the asset coordinate system, $+Y_{\text{local}}$ is the tank/back, and $-Y_{\text{local}}$ is the bowl/front.
  3. At rotation $Z = 0^\circ$, local $+Y$ points along world $+Y$ (towards the center of the room), and local $-Y$ points along world $-Y$ (into the South wall).
  4. Because the South wall's inward normal is $+Y_{\text{world}}$, the back of any fixture placed along the South wall must face $-Y_{\text{world}}$, and the front must face $+Y_{\text{world}}$.
- **Generic Architectural Rule**:
  - For any fixture mounted against or oriented toward a room boundary wall with inward normal $\vec{N}_{\text{wall}}$:
    - Fixture back vector ($+Y_{\text{local}}$) must align with $-\vec{N}_{\text{wall}}$.
    - Fixture front vector ($-Y_{\text{local}}$) must align with $+\vec{N}_{\text{wall}}$.
  - Along the South wall ($\vec{N}_{\text{wall}} = (0, +1, 0)$), this requires a $180^\circ$ ($\pi$ rad) rotation around $Z$.
  - Along the North wall ($\vec{N}_{\text{wall}} = (0, -1, 0)$), rotation is $0^\circ$.
  - Along the West wall ($\vec{N}_{\text{wall}} = (+1, 0, 0)$), rotation is $-90^\circ$ ($270^\circ$).
  - Along the East wall ($\vec{N}_{\text{wall}} = (-1, 0, 0)$), rotation is $+90^\circ$.

## D. Room Geometry Diagnosis

- **Physical Dimensions**:
  - Actual room bounds are $2.4384\text{ m} \times 1.8288\text{ m} \times 2.7432\text{ m}$ ($8\text{ ft} \times 6\text{ ft} \times 9\text{ ft}$). These dimensions are completely valid for an upscale urban residential bathroom.
- **Why the Room Felt Cramped**:
  1. **Keyhole Doorway Occlusion**: The previous renderer constructed solid 10cm jambs leaving only an 88cm door opening. Framing the room from outside this doorway occluded over 60% of the interior, creating a claustrophobic box view.
  2. **Lack of Fixture Zoning & Negative Space**: In the Lookbook references (pages 14, 16, 24, 26, 30), bathrooms feel spacious because the floor plane is uninterrupted, floating cabinetry exposes continuous floor surface beneath, and a transparent glass partition separates wet and dry zones without solid visual blockages.
  3. **Visual Depth & Continuity**: Large-format stone tiles with subtle continuous grout lines expand the perceived floor area.

---

## E. Shadow & Artifact Diagnosis

- **Diagonal Artifacts on Walls**:
  - A directional light placed high in the front corner cast sharp shadows of the North door jambs and lintel across the South feature wall, cutting dark diagonal bands across the plaster.
  - The cantilevered console shelf cast an oblique diagonal shadow up the back wall due to the steep off-center directional angle.
- **Ceiling / Wall Burn & Edge Falloff**:
  - Omnidirectional `pointLight` sources positioned $5\text{ cm}$ below the ceiling plane created harsh radial falloff on upper wall corners.
- **The Correct Lighting Setup**:
  - Set `castShadow={false}` on the camera/entrance wall so doorway geometry never cuts dark shapes across the interior.
  - Use downward-directed spotlights with soft cone penumbras for recessed ceiling fixtures.
  - Calibrate directional key light with positive shadow bias ($+0.0003$) and soft fill/contact shadows to ground fixtures naturally without dark diagonal geometry artifacts.

---

## F. Camera Diagnosis

- Camera positioning at $Y=1.65\text{ m}$ placed the viewpoint in the hallway outside the doorway.
- When orbit controls rotated, the camera clipped the door jamb walls.
- The ideal interior hero camera:
  - Positioned inside the room entrance threshold ($Y \approx 0.75\text{ m}$ to $0.80\text{ m}$, $Z \approx 1.30\text{ m}$ to $1.35\text{ m}$).
  - Framing the full spatial composition: grounded toilet on one side, floating vanity and mirror in the center, and shower zone with ceiling rainhead on the other side.
  - FOV calibrated to $72^\circ$ for an editorial interior perspective that emphasizes room boundaries and generous circulation.

---

## G. Proposed Generic Fixes

1. **Generic Fixture Orientation & Grounding**:
   - Establish semantic orientation based on mounting wall normal ($\vec{N}_{\text{wall}}$), aligning front vector into the room and back vector against the wall.
   - Ground fixtures with stable pivot at base contact ($Z=0$).
2. **Coherent Architectural Shell**:
   - Continuous uninterrupted floor slab with refined large-format stone texture and perimeter skirting.
   - Clean wall corners with consistent inward normals and zero z-fighting.
   - Full-height frameless glass shower partition separating the shower zone from the dry zone.
3. **Architectural Floating Vanity & Mirror**:
   - Floating vanity console supporting the vessel basin at ergonomic presentation height ($0.72\text{ m}$ counter, $\approx 0.88\text{ m}$ rim).
   - Architectural LED backlit mirror placeholder above the vanity.
4. **Balanced Layered Lighting**:
   - Downward recessed ceiling spotlights, soft ambient fill, and contact shadowing under grounded fixtures.
